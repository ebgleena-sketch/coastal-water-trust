HOMEPAGE

 

LEENA FOR MOULTON NIGUEL

Trust. Water. Community.

A fresh perspective focused on reliable water, responsible spending, and the future of our community.

I’m Leena Ray, a Moulton Niguel community member, mother, and former business leader running for Moulton Niguel Water District Director.

I’m not a career politician or a water-industry insider.

I’m a resident who believes our community deserves a Board that asks questions, listens to experts, protects ratepayers, and plans responsibly for the future.

My Commitment

Protect our water.
Protect our investments.
Protect our ratepayers.

Moulton Niguel has already invested heavily in water treatment, filtration, recycling, storage and infrastructure. I believe we should maximize the value of what residents have already paid for while carefully evaluating every new investment.

Our water future requires both innovation and discipline.

We need to continue exploring recycled water, advanced treatment, water reliability and conservation—but we also need to ask the difficult questions:

What problem are we solving?
What will it cost?
What alternatives were considered?
What are the long-term obligations?
And how does this benefit our ratepayers?

WATER QUALITY & CONFIDENCE

Safe water is the foundation of a healthy community.

MNWD reports that its drinking water meets state and federal standards and conducts approximately 12,000 water-quality tests each year.

I believe meeting those standards is essential.

But I also believe compliance should be the floor, not the ceiling.

Residents should have confidence in their water and understand how it is tested, treated, delivered and monitored.

I will advocate for transparency, continued investment in treatment and filtration, responsible evaluation of emerging technologies, and clear communication with residents.

WATER SECURITY

Moulton Niguel imports 100% of its potable water through the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California. Those supplies primarily originate from the Colorado River and the State Water Project.

That makes long-term water reliability especially important.

At the same time, MNWD already produces recycled water that meets approximately 25% of the District's total water demand.

I believe we should continue building on what is already working.

Use more of what we already have.
Explore responsible new technologies.
Strengthen local reliability.
Plan before a crisis.

RESPONSIBLE STEWARDSHIP

Water infrastructure requires enormous investments.

MNWD reports more than $2 billion in water and wastewater infrastructure assets and invested approximately $54 million in capital infrastructure in 2025.

That is a tremendous responsibility.

Every major project should be evaluated not only on whether it is technically possible, but whether it is financially responsible and provides measurable value to residents.

The District's 2019 dispute involving the South Orange County Wastewater Authority resulted in a $4.8 million settlement. That experience should reinforce the importance of due diligence, transparency, clearly defined obligations, and strong protections for ratepayers before entering major regional commitments.

We can work with regional partners while still protecting our community's interests.

TRANSPARENT LEADERSHIP

I believe residents deserve to understand:

What the District is planning

Why a project is necessary

What alternatives were considered

How much it will cost

How it could affect future rates

What risks exist

And how success will be measured

Government should not be mysterious.

My approach is simple: listen, learn, ask questions, explain clearly, and follow through.

A DIFFERENT KIND OF LEADERSHIP

My professional background is in marketing, business consulting, strategy, customer relationships and communications.

I have spent my career helping organizations understand problems, identify opportunities, communicate effectively and make better decisions.

I also understand that being a Water District Director does not mean pretending to be the engineer, scientist or financial expert in the room.

It means making sure the right questions are asked.

It means listening to qualified professionals.

It means understanding the numbers.

And it means remembering who ultimately pays for the decisions being made.

I’M RUNNING BECAUSE I CARE ABOUT WHERE WE ARE GOING.

I believe Moulton Niguel has a strong foundation.

My goal isn't to tear down what is working.

It is to build on it.

Protect the infrastructure we've already invested in.

Continue improving water quality and reliability.

Expand responsible water reuse.

Evaluate new investments carefully.

Protect ratepayers.

And keep the focus where it belongs:

On the people who live here.

My Priorities Page

MY PRIORITIES

1. WATER QUALITY & CONFIDENCE

Safe drinking water is non-negotiable.

MNWD currently reports that its drinking water meets state and federal standards and conducts approximately 12,000 water-quality tests annually.

I support that commitment—and I believe we should continue asking how we can do even better.

Residents should have access to understandable information about where their water comes from, how it is treated, how it is tested and what those results mean.

Compliance is essential. Confidence is the goal.

2. WATER RELIABILITY

Moulton Niguel imports all of its potable water through MWDOC from Metropolitan Water District of Southern California. Those supplies primarily come from the Colorado River and the State Water Project.

We cannot control droughts, climate conditions or decisions made by other agencies.

But we can control how prepared we are.

That means supporting:

Responsible water recycling

Advanced treatment technologies

Conservation

Infrastructure maintenance

Storage and system resilience

Regional cooperation when it benefits our community

Long-term water supply planning

We should prepare before we need the water—not after.

3. MAXIMIZE WHAT WE ALREADY OWN

MNWD has invested billions of dollars in water and wastewater infrastructure.

That investment belongs to the community.

Before approving expensive new projects, I want the District to clearly evaluate whether we can:

Repair it.
Upgrade it.
Modernize it.
Optimize it.
Or use it more efficiently.

New isn't automatically better.

Sometimes the smartest investment is getting more value from what we already have.

4. EXPAND RESPONSIBLE WATER REUSE

Recycled water already provides approximately 25% of MNWD's total water demand.

The District is also investing in advanced treatment technologies, including a $34.5 million Salinity Management Facility using ultrafiltration and reverse osmosis.

I support continuing to evaluate opportunities to increase local water reuse when they are technically sound, environmentally responsible and financially justified.

Every successful local water source can reduce pressure on imported supplies.

5. PROTECT RATEPAYERS

Water infrastructure is expensive—and mistakes are expensive too.

The District's 2019 dispute involving SOCWA resulted in a $4.8 million settlement.

That experience should remind us that major regional commitments require:

Thorough due diligence

Transparent financial analysis

Clear contractual obligations

Appropriate voting protections

Defined responsibilities

Meaningful exit provisions

Ongoing oversight

I support regional cooperation.

But cooperation should never mean writing a blank check.

Partner when it makes sense. Protect the ratepayer every time.

6. TRANSPARENCY & ACCOUNTABILITY

Residents should not have to be experts in water policy to understand what their District is doing.

I want information to be easier to understand and easier to access.

Before major decisions, residents should be able to understand:

What are we doing?
Why are we doing it?
What will it cost?
What alternatives exist?
What are the risks?
How will we know whether it worked?

That is accountability.

7. COMMUNITY FIRST

The Water District should be focused on providing reliable water and responsible service—not becoming a stepping stone for political ambition.

I believe Directors should remain focused on the people they were elected to serve.

**This isn't about politics.

It's about water.**

WHERE DOES OUR WATER COME FROM?

A journey that begins hundreds of miles away.

Most of us turn on the faucet without thinking about the extraordinary journey our water has taken to get there.

Moulton Niguel imports all of its potable water through the Municipal Water District of Orange County from the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California.

The primary imported sources are:

THE STATE WATER PROJECT

Water from Northern California travels through the California Aqueduct and other infrastructure before reaching Southern California.

THE COLORADO RIVER

Water travels approximately 242 miles through the Colorado River Aqueduct before reaching Southern California.

THEN IT COMES TO US

Imported water is treated at regional facilities, including the Diemer Filtration Plant in Yorba Linda and Baker Water Treatment Plant in Lake Forest, before being delivered through the regional transmission system into MNWD's service area.

That is an extraordinary system—and it also highlights why water reliability matters.

We cannot control the weather in Northern California.

We cannot control the Colorado River.

We cannot control every decision made by regional or state agencies.

But we can control how well we plan.

BUILDING A MORE RESILIENT FUTURE

MNWD already produces recycled water that meets approximately 25% of total water demand.

The District also operates advanced wastewater treatment facilities and continues evaluating opportunities to expand highly treated wastewater reuse.

My goal is not to suggest that one technology will solve everything.

Our water future should be diversified, carefully planned and financially responsible.

Use imported water wisely.

Maximize recycled water.

Invest in infrastructure.

Evaluate advanced treatment.

Strengthen conservation.

Plan for emergencies.

And always ask whether the next investment provides real value to the people paying for it.
